# Five-year stakeholder presentation options

Planning proposal, 30 September 2026. Developed with three GPT-6.1 Sol agents covering timelines, comparative views, and executive briefings. Assumption: mixed executive and investor audience; a short meeting with optional deeper diligence. No new company research was collected for this proposal.

The presentation should give people a small, stable set of questions to follow over time. Retain complete evidence in the ledger and progressively reveal it when requested. The current Alphabet pack is a design demonstration, not a completed five-year assessment: it covers FY2024 plus an April 2025 letter, with 84 extraction records, 13 curated economic statements supported by 18 records, and 66 uncurated records. Interpretations remain proposed.

## Four plans

| Plan | Presentation budget | Primary question | Best use | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Major-claims timeline | 15–20 turning points across five years, grouped into 8–12 histories | What did management say, change, and deliver? | Main stakeholder story; interactive presentation | Chronology can crowd out comparisons; curate carefully and offer year/theme filters. |
| B. Five-year comparison matrix | Five fiscal-year columns and 6–8 stable issue rows | Is delivery or performance improving, deteriorating, or still unknown? | Investment committee and recurring review | Dense on a slide; split into thematic sheets or show one dimension at a time. |
| C. Executive briefing | Six pages, at most eight featured economic statements | What should stakeholders understand and investigate? | 10–15 minute meeting, PDF or deck | Compresses detail; every takeaway needs a direct route to the supporting record. |
| D. Decision case studies | Three cases, one or two pages per case | How did an important decision or commitment play out? | Board discussion and deeper diligence | Selected examples alone cannot establish a representative overall record. |

## Plan A: major-claims timeline

Start with a five-year overview and a small number of material turning points. Use a readable year-by-year layout, with theme filters and an expandable vertical view on smaller screens. An ordinal layout must say that spacing does not represent elapsed time. Show coverage gaps in the year strip.

Each selected claim opens a connected history:

**Original statement → revision or reaffirmation → comparable result → current assessment**

The collapsed card contains only the topic, date/period, actor, and one-sentence status. The expanded record shows original and revised targets together, evidence on both sides, matching rationale and sources. Board decisions and company outcomes have distinct labels. A decision without a promise does not enter a promise-delivery sequence.

Use 15–20 turning points as a starting presentation budget, not a completeness claim or quota per year. Keep 8–12 major histories in view, with overflow accessible by theme. Distinct annual commitments can share a topic history but retain their own year, economic ID, target and assessment. Five annual capex plans are not one promise. Repeated mentions of the same promise are linked references.

Grounded example: **April 2025 letter: approximately $75B FY2025 capex plan → outcome not in this evidence pack.** The measurement year and statement month remain distinct. The April 2024 buyback authorization is a board decision; the $62B FY2024 repurchase total is company spending with no automatically asserted link to that authorization.

## Plan B: five-year comparison matrix

Rows contain stable economic questions; columns contain the last five completed issuer fiscal years. Use 6–8 rows, grouped under the four separate dimensions: delivery credibility, disclosure/accountability, operating efficiency and capital stewardship. For presentation slides, split the matrix into thematic sheets with two rows each.

A cell has one short statement, a textual evidence status, and at most one target/actual pair. Delivery comparisons sit in their target measurement year, with original statement date available separately. Operating results can show a comparable trend without a delivery verdict. A result discussed in multiple dimensions is linked, not counted as multiple achievements.

Selecting a cell reveals its source-backed history. Mark gaps explicitly: missing year coverage, outcome not found, not comparable, needs review, or unresolved. A metric-definition or accounting-basis change visibly breaks the series. Do not connect incompatible points or fill missing years with estimates.

Grounded example: FY2024 operating margin of 32%, compared with 27% in 2023 in the saved table, can be shown as company performance with legal/office-charge context. It does not establish delivery against an earlier promise or a director's contribution.

## Plan C: six-page executive briefing

1. **Summary:** three material takeaways, evidence cutoff, five-year coverage strip and important limits.
2. **Timeline:** selected decisions, original claims, revisions and outcomes.
3. **Delivery:** a compact table of the most consequential comparable targets and results.
4. **Accountability:** material corrections, withdrawals, adverse evidence and persistent unknowns.
5. **Economics:** separate sections for operating efficiency and capital stewardship, with a few comparable trends.
6. **Questions:** what remains unresolved, evidence needed and implications for further diligence.

Feature at most eight economic statements across the briefing. Reuse their stable IDs across pages so repetition is intentional context, not extra evidence. Link the timeline and source register for discussion beyond the six pages.

## Plan D: three decision case studies

Where evidence supports them, choose one consequential delivery success, one miss/revision/withdrawal, and one material unresolved or contested case. Disclose a missing category rather than inventing an example to balance the story. Cases are illustrations, not a delivery-rate sample.

Each case follows what was known at the time, the original promise or decision, later changes, comparable outcomes, adverse evidence and a bounded assessment. Put the plain-language story on the first page and sources, attribution and competing explanations on an optional second page.

Company outcomes remain separate from management choices, collective board decisions and documented individual contributions. Individual profiles need an actual role and tenure plus contribution evidence. Spending within plan and producing good investment returns are different questions.

## Recommended combination

Make **Plan A the main experience**, preceded by a one-page summary and followed by optional evidence drilldowns. Offer Plan B as a second view over the same selected histories. Export Plan C for stakeholder meetings; use Plan D when discussion requires depth.

The default reading path is: **one-page summary → 15–20 major turning points → selected claim history → exact evidence**. The searchable full register remains available to analysts. The six-page briefing is an export of this selection, not another independently maintained collection.

## What earns a place

Apply the same selection policy across all years before choosing favorable examples:

- Prioritize financial materiality relative to the issuer, strategic importance, stakeholder consequences, and meaningful changes in a commitment or its outcome. Agree issuer-appropriate thresholds before populating the five-year view.
- Include all material misses, withdrawals, adverse findings and persistent unresolved commitments in either the main view or an explicitly linked exceptions list. The visible-card budget is not a reason to hide an adverse case.
- Merge repeated mentions of the same economic statement. Keep distinct scopes, annual targets and genuine revisions separate. Broad aspirations belong in context unless measurable commitments are established.
- Show financial results as a few comparable trends instead of one card for each repeated annual statistic. Headcount alone is not productivity; authorization alone is not spending; spending alone is not return on capital.
- Keep an inclusion/exclusion log with reasons and a coverage strip. A missing outcome is not a miss. Proposed interpretation is not reviewed analysis. Use text labels as well as color.
- Preserve the four assessment dimensions separately. Do not calculate a universal trust score or a delivery percentage without a defensible reviewed population and comparability rules.

## Maintaining five years without presentation growth

Use a rolling window of five completed fiscal years, with current-year updates explicitly separated. Keep an older promise visible when it is still material and unresolved or has an outcome inside the window. Opening dates can precede the displayed window; label this rather than losing the origin.

For each update, append evidence and versions, refresh the fixed overview, and show a short changes-since-last-review list: new material statement, revision, withdrawal, comparable result, restatement, or reopened uncertainty. Unchanged annual wording adds citations, not new headline cards. Save each dated presentation snapshot and its selected IDs so historical views remain reproducible.

Implementation can add a versioned presentation-selection file over the existing ledger: displayed fiscal window, cutoff, selected entry/version IDs, topic histories, ordering, inclusion reasons and linked exceptions. Matching logic stays in the ledger. The existing React timeline can later render this selection; no additional collector or model runtime is required for presentation.

## Before a real five-year presentation

Complete the annual-report coverage for the selected five fiscal years, then follow material original statements to their revisions and comparable outcomes. Earlier sources may be required for promises originating before the window. Add quarterly, proxy or other primary evidence where an annual report cannot resolve timing, governance responsibility or an outcome. Keep source coverage distinct from assessment direction.

This proposal does not launch collection or infer the missing years. The existing [ledger contract](../src/credibility/LEDGER.md) and [presentation direction](../data/live-alphabet-2024/presentation-direction.md) supply the evidence, date, attribution and review requirements.
